How to run the test

  1. Cover everything. Name, logo, product shot, tagline, web address. If a mascot or colour is the thing you are testing, leave it showing and cover the rest.
  2. Show it to category buyers. People who buy what you sell, not colleagues and not your followers. Ask "who made this?" and write down the answer word for word.
  3. Count. How many named you, how many named someone else, how many did not know. Fame is the share who name you. Uniqueness is how many of the namers named only you.
  4. Score it. Put the counts in assetscan, which draws the distinctive asset grid.

Why this test

Distinctive assets

A distinctive asset is something people link to your brand without seeing the name: a colour, a shape, a sound, a character. Ehrenberg-Bass research (Romaniuk, Building Distinctive Brand Assets, 2018) scores assets on fame, how many people link the asset to the brand, and uniqueness, how many link it only to that brand.

Covering the brand is the quickest way to see what an ad is carrying without the name. Most ads fail this and the team finds out after the spend.

Why a redaction bar and not a blur

A blur or a pixelate can be reversed by squinting, and logos survive it more often than you would think. A solid bar removes the pixels. Everything under it is gone from the exported file.

Read this before you trust the answers

A few friends is not a test. People who know you will guess your brand. You need category buyers, and enough of them that one lucky guess does not move the result.

This tool does not find logos for you. You draw the bars. Anything you miss stays visible and will skew the answers, so look at the export before you send it.

One ad is one asset set. A good result for one execution says nothing about the brand's other ads.
